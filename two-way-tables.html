Two-Way Tables: And / Or

    Find P(A), P(B), P(A and B), and P(A or B).
    P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)

    Outcomes that are in both A and B (the striped ones) get counted once when you find P(A) and again when you find P(B). That's double counting. Subtract P(A and B) one time so each outcome is only counted once.

    Every fraction uses the same bottom number: the total number of outcomes.

    The dashed purple number is the grand total.

      Find P(A), P(B), P(A and B), and P(A or B).
      P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)

      P(A) = the row total ÷ the grand total. P(B) = the column total ÷ the grand total.

      P(A and B) = the one cell where the row and column cross ÷ the grand total.

      For P(A or B), the crossing cell is inside the row total and the column total, so adding them counts it twice. Subtract it once.
